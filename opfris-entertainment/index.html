AI in media and entertainment in 2026

Two years after this article first appeared, the question is no longer whether AI belongs in entertainment, but on what terms.

Hollywood has started to write those terms into its contracts. In April 2026, members of the Writers Guild of America ratified a new agreement with the studios that runs until May 2030. It keeps the AI protections won in 2023 and adds a new rule: studios must notify the Guild in writing if they license writers’ work to train a commercial generative AI system, after which the Guild can ask to discuss the licence, including pay for writers.

In music, the volume of AI output is exploding while the audience stays small. Deezer reports that around 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks were uploaded per day at the peak in June 2026, more than half of all new music it receives. Yet these tracks account for only 1 to 3 percent of listening on the platform, and up to 85 percent of their streams in 2025 turned out to be fraudulent.

Regulation is catching up as well. Since 2 August 2026, the transparency rules in Article 50 of the EU AI Act apply: deepfakes must be disclosed. For evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional works the rule is lighter, as long as the disclosure does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work. In June 2026, the European Commission published a voluntary code of practice that shows how to mark and label AI-generated content.
