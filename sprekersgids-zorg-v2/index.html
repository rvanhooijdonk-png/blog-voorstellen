Keynote speaker on AI and the future of healthcare: what should they bring to your conference?

A good keynote on AI and the future of healthcare shows where AI is already being used in healthcare, what is still being tested, and what both could mean for your staff and your organisation. Use the seven questions below to find the speaker who can do that for your room.

You are organising a healthcare conference, perhaps for hospital boards, a medical association, insurers or a technology supplier. Everyone wants AI on the programme. The hard part is finding a speaker who does more than list new tools: someone who makes the future concrete and helps people see what to do next.

Where AI in healthcare is heading

Some AI tools are already used in care; others are still being tested. The biggest change is in how people work together.

The direction is clear. On 29 October 2026 the 53 Member States of the WHO European Region unanimously adopted the Region's first roadmap for AI in health, covering 2026 to 2033. WHO/Europe says AI can help detect diseases earlier, alert health workers when a patient's condition deteriorates, reduce the administrative burden on clinicians and give people answers to their first questions about their symptoms, day or night.

You can already see this in practice. AI reviews scans next to the radiologist, drafts the notes while the doctor talks to the patient and helps plan beds and shifts. Other uses are still in pilots, such as AI agents that answer the phone and book appointments, or chatbots that guide patients between therapy sessions. Some patients already show their test results to a chatbot before they see their doctor.

The biggest change is not technical. Using these tools changes who checks the result, who makes the final decision, what teams need to learn and which decisions boards can no longer postpone. The WHO roadmap reflects that: next to ethics and regulation, it puts governance and leadership, data and digital infrastructure, and workforce skills at the centre.

After a good AI session, the audience knows what the next step in AI will be: which unexpected possibilities and major opportunities are coming, and how organisations, roles and jobs can adapt to them.

Seven questions to ask every speaker

Ask what the speaker will bring: a view of what is coming, examples from your sector and next steps for your audience.

1. Does the speaker show what is coming, not only what exists today?

Ask how they look ahead: which developments in AI and healthcare they expect in the next five to ten years, and how they back that up. A good keynote gives the audience a picture of the future they can act on.

2. Which concrete examples from healthcare will they use?

Ask for three recent examples from hospitals, primary care or mental health care, with the organisation and the source.

3. Do they separate what works now from what is still a promise?

The audience should leave knowing what is ready to use, what is being tested and what is mostly hype. Ask the speaker how they make that difference clear on stage.

4. What does it mean for the work of doctors, nurses and support staff?

Ask how the keynote deals with the people in care: which tasks change, which new skills are needed and how time freed up by AI can go back to patients.

5. What does it ask of leaders and boards?

AI raises questions about investment, data, responsibility and the shape of the organisation. Ask which decisions the speaker will put in front of your leaders, and how they help them choose.

6. How do they deal with trust and risk?

Patients and staff need to know when AI is used, how its answers are checked and who remains responsible. Ask how the speaker covers errors, bias and privacy, without turning the keynote into a list of warnings.

7. Will your audience know what to do next?

Ask what people can start, stop or decide the day after the session, and how the speaker tailors that to your programme, your audience and your format, whether it is on site, online or in English.

Frequently asked questions

Should the speaker be a clinician?

Not necessarily. What matters is that the speaker knows healthcare well enough to use examples your audience recognises. Decide first who the keynote is for, then ask each candidate how they have spoken to that audience before.

What does it cost?

Ask for the fee, travel, the recording policy and whether a Q&A is included. Our guide on how to choose an AI keynote speaker covers fees in more detail.

To sum up

Choose the speaker who makes the future of healthcare concrete for your audience.

A keynote on AI and the future of healthcare works when people leave with a clear view of what is coming and what it means for their work, their team and their organisation. Ask every candidate the seven questions and compare the answers.
